**Valid: 0 of the 30 A/B samples are void**, against a limit of 2. Every
sample has `post_power_limit_w = 150`, only `GpuIdle` throttles and
`post_clock_sm ≥ 1620` (the minimum, at sample 21). On every row the
power references, loadavg and both snapshots are present and non-NA. That
is #213's acceptance criterion, shown on a real run.

**What "valid" does not mean.** The snapshots are taken outside the timed
window. `GpuIdle` before and after a sample cannot show that the window
itself was free of throttling. Today's own pre-push gate saw `SwPowerCap`
at 150 W on another kernel, so in-window throttling does happen. The rules
can only reject a sample, never certify one.

## Deviations from the pre-registration

Both are defects in the pre-registration itself. Both could have been seen
before the run, and both are recorded here rather than silently worked
around.

1. **`order_in_run` cannot be a regressor within a measurement.** It is
   constant for each measurement (`sd64_explicit` is always 1, and so on).
   Position in the process can only be compared *across* measurements, and
   there it is fully confounded with the config, because the bench
   measures its six configs in a fixed order.
2. **Condition and `pre_pstate` are the same variable in this run.** All 15
   A samples entered at P0 (1770–1785 MHz) and all 15 B samples at P8
   (210 MHz). A regression with both terms is not estimable. The A-vs-B
   comparison below is therefore also the entry-state comparison.

The rest of the plan ran as written: burn-in excluded, results row by row,
load as a covariate, W samples for the lead-in.

## What this settles for #195

The gate measures `conv2d_implicit_gemm` as `sd64_implicit`: the same
executable, section `SD 64`, match `Implicit (single kern)` (see
`data/baselines.json`).

1. **The gate-like idle gap does not explain the variance.** The 90 s gap,
   with its P8 entry, has no detectable effect on any of the six
   measurements. For `sd64_implicit` it is +0.5%, with a 95% CI of
   −3.9% to +4.8% of B.
2. **`sd64_implicit` has intrinsic run-to-run spread at a fixed 150 W
   regime on a quiet host:** rel sd 5.7%, range 6788–8107 (1.19×), the same
   in both conditions. Its implicit siblings stay within 0.86% (`sd32`) and
   0.94% (`sd128`), about six times tighter.
3. **Whether the cause is SD64's shape or SD64's position cannot be told
   from this design.** SD64 is always the first config in the process
   (deviation 1).

All 30 samples clear the gate's 10% floor below the 7150 baseline (6435).
The baseline is a median of 5 from 2026-05-10 and sits about 5% below
today's mean of 7531.

## Exploratory (NOT pre-registered)

The `sd64_implicit` values looked like two clusters. The largest gap in
the sorted values is 166 GFLOPS (7611 to 7777) against a mean spacing of
about 45, which is suggestive but not conclusive at n = 30. Split there:
14 high (mean 7929) and 16 low (mean 7183), ratio 1.104.

**No recorded variable separates the two groups.**
- Condition: A 8 high / 7 low, B 6 high / 9 low.
- `post_clock_sm`: 1760 vs 1759.
- `post_temp_c`: 57.4 vs 57.4.
- `post_power_w`: 66.7 vs 67.5.
- `pre_load_1m`: 2.2 vs 1.5.
- The four smaller configs: equal.

The high/low sequence looks random (14 runs in 30 samples). Whatever moves
`sd64_implicit` happens inside the timed window, where the outside
snapshots cannot see it.

## Next steps (proposed, not run)

1. **In-window telemetry, symmetric across conditions.** A background
   `nvidia-smi --query-gpu=clocks.sm,clocks.mem,power.draw,clocks_throttle_reasons.active -lms 100`
   logger that runs for the whole session at a fixed rate. That loads A, B
   and W equally, unlike a poll scheduled into B's gaps. It would show
   whether the low `sd64_implicit` samples coincide with a clock dip or a
   throttle event inside the window.
2. **Separate position from config.** Let the bench take its config order
   from an argument (for example SD128 first, then SD64), and repeat with
   the order permuted.
